# IDYLLIC LIGHTING

## Website requirements & creative direction
**Cursor handoff | Version 1.0 | November 1, 2026**

> **BEAUTIFUL AFTER DARK**
> Build the house. Invite the scroll. Bring it to light.

Create a first website for Idyllic Lighting, not a redesign of an existing site. The visitor sees a house assemble from the ground up into a dim nighttime scene. One roof-edge bulb gently pulses. Scrolling extends the illumination along the roofline while the headline gives way to useful business information.

Keep the result recognizable, warm and credible. The hero is the signature experience; the rest of the site should make services, examples and inquiries easy to understand.

### The two conversion paths
**Get a Free Quote** and **Request a Site Visit** must be usable immediately. Both open the same inquiry system with the correct request type selected. A preferred visit date is a request, never a confirmed appointment.

### Non-negotiable decisions
- Use a real Remotion composition for the hero, with native scrolling controlling the lighting reveal.
- Start illumination at the far-left roof edge. Previously lit bulbs stay on; reverse scrolling retraces the same route.
- Use a brief ground-up assembly, not a blocking loading screen. Two gentle idle pulses, then a steady light.
- Keep business content and controls in normal HTML. Provide static and reduced-motion alternatives.
- Use authentic, approved installation photos for the portfolio. Label the animated house as a visualization when it is not documentary photography.
- Never invent reviews, availability, prices, credentials, addresses or project history.

### How to use this handoff
Read sections 2-3 for evidence and sources; 4-8 for design, motion and assets; 9-12 for functionality and build standards; 13-15 for skills, testing and the Cursor kickoff prompt. Sections 16-17 contain technical reference links.

**Status language:** Published reference = information a cited source lists, not owner approval. Proposed = a design/build instruction. Owner approval required = a launch gate. All design copy is proposed unless identified otherwise.

# 04 / VISUAL DIRECTION

## Beautiful After Dark
**Proposed character:** architectural, warm, confident and residential. Premium presentation without pretending the business is a national franchise or a luxury-only installer.

### Colour system
| Token | Value | Intended use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Night | #0B0E13 | Hero, navigation and footer |
| Charcoal | #171B21 | Secondary dark surfaces |
| Warm ivory | #F5F1E8 | Reading sections and light text |
| Champagne gold | #D8B76A | Light accents and key actions |
| Soft grey | #D7D9DE | Supporting text on dark surfaces |

Test actual text/background pairs for contrast. Gold is an accent, not the default body-text colour. Preserve the approved logo; do not casually replace it with a newly generated wordmark.

### Type and composition
Use an expressive but legible serif for major headings and a clean sans-serif for body copy. Suggested starting pair: **Cormorant Garamond Semibold + Manrope**. Verify font licences; self-host only permitted files. Use no more than two families and a small weight range.

Compose the house as the focal point, with a stable text area beside or above it and a restrained gradient behind text. Use generous margins and editorial spacing. Design the mobile crop separately; do not crop away the first bulb or the important roof peaks.

### What the site must not become
No neon gaming palette, spinning 3D house, stock mansion slideshow, particle storm, snow overlay, glitter cursor, bouncing buttons, scroll hijacking or repeated long pinned sections. Do not combine every animation technique in the same viewport.

### Seasonal positioning
Make warm-white illumination the year-round opening. Place Christmas/holiday messaging in an editable campaign section below it. Use owner-approved seasonal photos and a holiday inquiry option, not a permanently Christmas-only identity.

Set campaign start/end dates and an evergreen fallback in content configuration. Never show expired countdowns, unsupported installation deadlines or invented limited availability.

### Proposed navigation
Logo | Services | Our Work | How It Works | Contact | **Get a Free Quote**
Keep **Request a Site Visit** visible in the hero and inquiry section. On small screens use a compact header and accessible menu; avoid a floating bar that covers form fields.

# 05 / THE OPENING STORY

## Let the visitor switch the house on
### A. Arrival - ground-up assembly
Show the nighttime setting immediately. Assemble the foundation, facade and roof into the same complete house in approximately **1.0-1.5 seconds**. Use layered masks and small upward reveals, not flying bricks or construction machinery. Maintain a stable camera and convincing architectural alignment.

The business identity and inquiry controls remain available while the scene enters. This is an entrance animation, not a loading percentage or a requirement before using the page.

### B. Idle - the first bulb
The completed house remains visible in dim ambient light. All decorative lights are off except the bulb at the **far-left edge of the roof**. Give it two gentle warm pulses, finishing within a short idle cue, then hold it steadily. Keep the combined automatic intro and cue under five seconds; do not loop them indefinitely.

**Proposed opening copy:**
> Every transformation starts with a little light.
> Permanent & seasonal lighting for your home and business.
> Get a Free Quote | Request a Site Visit
> Scroll to bring it to life.

### C. Scroll - illumination travels
On downward scrolling, stop the idle pulse and extend the light from that first bulb along an explicitly authored roofline route. Follow corners and peaks, not a rectangular left-to-right screen wipe. Already illuminated bulbs stay on. Add local light spill on soffits and nearby surfaces without brightening the entire sky or photograph.

Stopping freezes progress. Upward scrolling reverses the same illumination sequence, but never dismantles the house. No automatic chase-light loop.

### D. Business introduction - synchronized text
As the lights progress, lift the original headline upward and fade it out. Bring the introduction up from below into the same text region. Use only three readable copy states, with still periods between changes.

**Middle state:** Meet Idyllic Lighting. Follow with one short, owner-approved description of their services and service area.

**Completed state:** Let's bring your home to light. Repeat the two inquiry actions. Hold the fully lit scene before normal page content resumes.

### Bypass behaviour
Immediate scrolling, a section link or either inquiry action skips the assembly and aligns the scene with the current scroll position. Returning from the form does not replay the introduction. Provide an explicit Skip animation link. For reduced motion, show a fully lit static house and all essential content in normal flow.

# 06 / REMOTION & SCROLL ARCHITECTURE

## One controller, two kinds of time
Use **Remotion + @remotion/player** for the composited house, masks, bulbs and light spill. Use **Motion useScroll** or an existing equivalent to read native page progress. Keep HTML text and controls outside the visual composition. Follow the current official APIs. [T1-T5]

### State machine
**INTRO -> IDLE -> SCRUB -> COMPLETE**, with separate loading/failure and reduced-motion guards. Keep the house assembled once the intro finishes or is skipped. Store an intro-seen flag for the current tab session; safely handle unavailable storage.

A single controller owns the Player. It advances the brief intro/idle cue, then pauses and seeks during SCRUB. Do not let autoplay, a requestAnimationFrame timer and scroll all fight over the same frames. Stop background work when hidden or offscreen.

### Proposed timeline
Reserve a short intro segment, a finite idle segment and a separate illumination segment. A starting point is **36 intro frames + 72 idle frames + 300 scrub frames at 30 fps**. These are design parameters, not a forced visit duration. Recompute if the art direction changes.

During SCRUB:
```
p = clamp(stickySectionProgress, 0, 1)
frame = scrubStart + round(p * (scrubFrameCount - 1))
player.pause()
player.seekTo(frame)
```
This is implementation pseudocode, not a complete component. Scope subscriptions; seek only when the rounded target changes. Keep composition visuals deterministic from frame data and avoid whole-page rerenders. [T1-T3]

### Bulb path and text synchronization
Store normalized bulb coordinates, route order and spill masks in one configuration. Order bulbs by authored path distance, including an explicit branch order for different roof levels. Ramp each bulb softly around its activation threshold. A lit bulb stays on until reverse progress crosses that threshold.

Suggested scroll bands: opening 0-15%; first text transition 15-25%; introduction 25-65%; second transition 65-75%; completed message 75-100%. Complete illumination around 85% and hold. Adjust after reading/interaction tests.

### Layout and fallbacks
Start with a desktop wrapper around 250svh including a sticky scene; tune for clarity. Do not multiply pinning across the whole site. Use one shared coordinate transform for the house and lights; recalculate after resizing. Keep a static poster until assets are decoded. If the Player fails, retain the poster, copy and links without a blank hero. Inspect Remotion licensing for both developer and client usage before release. [T6]

# 07 / IMAGE & HERO-ASSET PRODUCTION

## Prepare the artwork before polishing the animation
### Asset priorities
**Brand:** original logo in SVG or transparent PNG, approved colours and any brand guidelines.

**Portfolio:** owner-approved original installation photographs. Prefer full-house nighttime views, close-ups of mounting quality, seasonal examples and genuine event work. Obtain landscape and portrait crops where possible. Ask for original files rather than social-media screenshots.

**Hero:** one coherent house scene, with the same architecture, lens, crop and lighting across all layers. A real commissioned/approved photograph is preferred. An illustrated or generated scene may be used as a clearly identified lighting visualization, never as proof of a completed Idyllic installation.

The house in the existing logo is a style reference, not a ready-made high-resolution architectural asset. Do not extract it and enlarge it into a blurry full-screen image.

### Required layers and metadata
| Asset | Purpose |
| --- | --- |
| Night background | Stable sky and surroundings throughout |
| House base and masks | Foundation, facade and roof assembly |
| Unlit complete house | Seamless handoff into the idle scene |
| Roofline/bulb configuration | Explicit coordinates and activation route |
| Local glow/spill layers | Convincing nearby illumination, not global exposure |
| Fully lit poster | Static, reduced-motion and error fallback |
| Mobile crop configuration | Keeps roofline and starting bulb in view |

Use a common master coordinate system. Export web-sized AVIF/WebP assets plus suitable fallbacks; retain editable masters outside the public deployment. Inspect halos and transparent edges against the final dark background.

### Image treatment rules
Correct exposure, crop, noise and white balance conservatively. Do not add nonexistent installation features, replace customers' houses, fake before-and-after evidence or use competitors' work. Keep originals and log edits. Get permission before publishing identifiable homes or details.

Maintain an asset manifest with source/permalink, owner, permission status, actual-project versus visualization, service category, edit history, crop/focal point and alt text. No asset enters the real-project gallery without approval.

### Acquisition workflow
Use B2 to locate posts and albums. Capture their real URLs and dates in the manifest; request full-resolution originals from the owner. Where access or permission is missing, create an explicit placeholder and continue the build. Do not hotlink Facebook CDN images, scrape private content or invent missing post links.

# 10 / BACKEND, DELIVERY & PRIVACY

## A form is finished only when the lead arrives reliably
### Proposed production architecture
Preserve a suitable existing stack. For a fresh project, use React + TypeScript with prerendered/server-rendered marketing content, a client-only hero island and a server-side inquiry endpoint. Next.js is one implementation option; do not rewrite an existing project solely to use it.

Use a maintained transaction-email provider and private object storage already approved for the project. Select hosting and providers only after project-owner approval; preserve any existing infrastructure and keep credentials server-side. A static-only preview cannot deliver a secure production form without a backend.

### Request lifecycle
Validate -> apply abuse controls -> create a durable lead and notification job -> acknowledge with a non-sensitive reference -> notify the owner -> monitor/retry failed notifications. Use idempotency protection against double-click and retried duplicate submissions. Track delivery separately from request acceptance; never promise inbox delivery solely because an API request was accepted.

Notify a configurable, owner-approved inbox. The public source email is a candidate, not permission to send test leads. Use a verified sender; use validated visitor email as Reply-To when present, not as an impersonated From address. Include request type, service and preferred visit window without exposing secrets or public upload links.

### Abuse prevention and validation
Use server-side schema validation, bounded text lengths, escaped output, rate limiting, a honeypot and a challenge mechanism where appropriate. Validate Turnstile tokens server-side when used; its client widget alone is insufficient. Protect cookie-authenticated admin actions appropriately. Never trust client-supplied recipient addresses, file paths or prices. [T12]

### Photo uploads
Proposed limit: up to 5 JPEG/PNG/WebP images, maximum 10 MB each, with a conservative total cap. Validate file signatures and actual decoding, not only extensions. Enforce pixel/dimension limits, re-encode safe derivatives, strip location metadata, randomize names and reject active formats. Keep objects private; use authenticated or short-lived access for staff. [T11]

### Data handling
Keep addresses, contact details and photos out of URLs, analytics payloads, public buckets and ordinary logs. Limit staff access. Agree a retention/deletion schedule and implement cleanup, including abandoned uploads. Provide a plain-language privacy notice describing collection, purpose, processors and a contact route. Keep API keys server-side and out of the repository.

# 11 / ACCESSIBILITY & PERFORMANCE

## Protect the experience when motion is not suitable
### Reduced motion and user control
Respect prefers-reduced-motion from first render and if it changes. Disable assembly, pulse, parallax, scrubbing and long pinned spacing. Show the fully illuminated poster and ordinary-flow copy. The animation is not essential to understanding the business. [T5, T8]

Provide Skip animation and a clear route to services/inquiries. The intro and idle cue must be finite; do not add unpausable continuous blinking. Avoid flashing effects altogether. Native scrolling must work with wheel, trackpad, touch and keyboard. [T7]

### Content and keyboard access
Use semantic headings, landmarks, real links/buttons, visible focus and labelled controls. Keep copy and forms outside Remotion. Treat decorative visual layers as hidden from assistive technology. Never announce every frame or automatically move focus on scroll.

Only the visible copy state's controls may be interactive. Do not leave invisible links in the tab order. Maintain a static accessible business summary so the scene's visual text changes do not remove information. On intentional navigation to the form, move focus appropriately to its heading without trapping the user.

Target comfortable 44 x 44 CSS-pixel controls as a project design standard. Test contrast, zoom, error messages, keyboard-only submission and a screen-reader smoke test. Do not call the site compliant solely because an automated check passes.

### Mobile behaviour
Compose at 360-390 px first, then tablet and desktop. Keep the starting bulb and roof readable. Shorten or remove pinning when the viewport cannot accommodate the house and text. A fully lit static mobile hero is preferable to a broken effect. Preserve both inquiry paths and all service information.

### Performance plan
Deliver HTML copy, inquiry links and a lightweight poster before loading the animation enhancement. Use responsive image variants, defined dimensions and deferred below-fold media. Avoid a full-screen autoplay MP4 and a heavyweight runtime 3D scene as default choices. Pause/unmount expensive work when appropriate; clean up subscriptions.

Treat initial media budgets as engineering targets: roughly 300 KB mobile poster, 500 KB desktop poster, and 2 MB additional hero assets after initial content. Measure rather than blindly compressing valuable detail.

Use Core Web Vitals goals of LCP <= 2.5 s, INP <= 200 ms and CLS <= 0.1, assessed at the 75th percentile once field data exists. Before launch, use repeatable lab checks; do not claim field results from a local test. [T13]
